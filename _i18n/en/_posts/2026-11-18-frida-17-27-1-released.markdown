A small one. The rest of our binaries now carry their version the way
Gadget does, two latent bugs in Idun are gone, found by building against
a GLib with its runtime checks enabled, and the XNU agent stopped sleeping
through two kinds of wakeups.

## Version metadata

In 17.27.0, Gadget started embedding its name, version and architectures,
readable through *BinaryMetadata.from_file()* without loading it.
frida-server, frida-portal, frida-inject and gum-graft now do the same, so
tools can tell which one they have without running it. The embedding moved
into a generator in our release engineering tooling, shared by frida-core
and frida-gum.

## Idun

The GLib in the binaries we ship is built without runtime checks, which
hid two bugs. They surfaced when building against a distro's GLib, where
those checks are typically enabled.

When talking to a jailed iOS device, our client for the Instruments
process control service is told when a process terminates. The
notification carries the pid as an *NSNumber*, which we read as if it were
a GObject. Without runtime checks, GLib hands back the pointer without
looking at its type, so this happened to work. With them it returns
*NULL*, and we crashed.

A handful of enum properties were also never installed there, as GLib
rejects a property whose default is not a member of its enum.

## Barebone

Getting the XNU agent's tests to run reliably on CI turned up two lost
wakeups, both a matter of ordering. The agent injected into a process
sampled its wake word after testing the predicate, so a wake landing in
between left it asleep with work already queued. And when that agent rang
the kernel half's bell before the kernel half's loop had blocked, the wake
was lost and the queued frame stranded. A test that wedges on CI now fails
within a time budget and dumps the host's stacks and the guest's
registers, rather than hanging the job.

Enjoy!
